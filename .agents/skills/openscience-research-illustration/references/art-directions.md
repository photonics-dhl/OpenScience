# Art directions

Choose for the role of the image and the user's request. These are design decisions to adapt, not locked templates. Keep scientific meanings and essential labels constant when comparing styles. Start from the laws below in every direction.

## Ground, frame and hierarchy laws (all directions)

- **One ground.** The entire canvas is one flat surface of one deliberately chosen colour, edge to edge. Never two backgrounds, never a panel or band with a different fill, never a vignette, halo or gradient falloff. If the picture is genuinely divided into panels, every panel still sits on the identical ground; the division comes from spacing and line work, never from a change of fill.
- **A divider must assert something true.** A rule, frame, box or band earns its place only when it marks a real source-supported boundary (a domain edge, a before/after step). "Top half / bottom half" splits, decorative rules and framing devices that merely organise the page are removed. If you cannot name the sourced meaning of a line, delete the line.
- **One focal point, one reading path.** Name the single element the eye lands on first, then the ordered path through the rest. Everything else is quieter in scale, weight and contrast.
- **Two text levels, fixed.** Primary labels (variable, category, boundary value) plus one smaller secondary level for essential qualifiers. No third decorative level; no text as ornament. One type family, one alignment logic.
- **Colour carries meaning; neutrals are chosen.** One meaning per colour. Keep axes, leaders and grids quieter than the subject. Pick the ground on purpose and tint neutrals toward the subject's own hue family. Do not fall back on the stock warm-cream ground with a single accent, or on pure white, without deciding the subject wants it; never mix a warm ground with a pure-white area in one picture.
- **Silence is part of the composition.** Most of the canvas stays quiet; detail concentrates at the focal relation.
- **Frames and panels must not invent apparatus.** Borders, boxes, badges, arrows and shadows must not imply a device, container or measurement the sources do not support.
- **A catalogue default is not a design.** When swapped-in layout or style galleries suggest multi-module grids (bento, dense modules, dashboards), treat them as available options, not as the requested structure. A single scientific relationship normally wants one continuous composition rather than a panelised infographic.
- **Judge at reading size.** Check the result at single-column width. If the ground reads as two pieces, or the hierarchy collapses, simplify the composition instead of adding elements.

## Scientific atlas

Use a purposeful focal diagram with crisp, restrained line weights and high-quality typography. Prefer white or a barely warm background, dark neutral labels and a small semantic accent palette. Scale the scientifically important relation generously. Put labels close to their subjects; use leaders only when placement cannot resolve ambiguity. Avoid the default appearance of a software screenshot, an empty chart scaffold or boxed summaries. Geometry remains precise; paper texture is optional and subordinate.

## Editorial illustration

Give one source-grounded subject a distinct silhouette and a clear place in the frame. Build hierarchy through subject scale, thoughtful empty space, a limited palette and a coherent material. Typography can be expressive when a title is actually part of the assignment; do not add a masthead. A material treatment may be painterly, printed, cut-paper or sculptural if it cannot be mistaken for unsupported apparatus or quantitative physics. Do not confuse dramatic color with a strong composition.

## Watercolor paper

Use translucent pigment, quiet warm paper and restrained blue/ochre or another intentional palette. Keep the essential outlines, boundaries and labels legible; let pigment variation decorate surfaces rather than encode field intensity. Balance a large focal form with carefully placed short text. When the user has selected a watercolor reference, preserve its actual lightness, edge treatment and visual rhythm through the attached image instead of forcing a generic beige background.

## Ink wash

Use ink value, deliberate empty space and a small accent to emphasize the focal relation. Preserve crisp scientific boundaries and restrained, readable lettering. Wash can shape the visual rhythm but cannot introduce an unsupported density, trajectory or topography. Avoid unrelated landscape or calligraphic decoration. This direction may be expressive or analytical according to the picture's purpose.

## Reading size

Judge the output at the size it will appear in the research page. The subject and labels should remain readable without magnification. If an image needs many equations or several domains to be understood, separate explanatory roles rather than relying on higher resolution to rescue an overloaded composition.
